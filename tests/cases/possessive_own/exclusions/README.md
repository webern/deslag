Own with no word after it is an idiom for "by itself". The worker runs on its
own. The users keep files of their own. A cache can hold its own. Nobody needs a
room of one's own. It is on its own, and that is fine. They stand on their very
own.

Own before a function word ends its phrase. It runs on its own in production,
checks its own for drift, builds on its own with no setup, and stops on its own
because it must.

After on, the idiom holds whatever follows. You are on your own now. On its own it
does nothing. It runs on their own anymore, on its own like a library, on its own
config and on its very own. Let's own it.

Other words and other shapes are not it. The repo owns the config, and its owner
reads it. Its ownership is clear. Users own their data. Own files are not
possessed by anyone.

Punctuation breaks the match: its own, config; its own: config; its own. Config.
Code is not prose: `its own config`, `the project's own docs`.
